Agentic POS: the merchant an agent can call

One counter, shared by staff and buyer agents, with a person in the loop when money needs judgment. A working sandbox, built to show the trust boundary before a live card is in the path.

“A POS is a human screen over a perishable good and a cash drawer.”

Who it is for

Partners wiring a buyer agent, operators watching policy, and anyone trying the sandbox. Text to order is how a person at the shelf hits the same rules. The product is hired by the people who have to trust an autonomous buyer.

  • Buyer-agent builders need a merchant they can call: public policy, a real quote, and a refusal they can retry, substitute, or halt on.
  • Store operators need to 86 an item, open and close the day, and approve the money exceptions a model must not decide.
  • Sandbox visitors need to see that boundary in a few minutes, with fake pay and a fixed demo clock, before anyone talks about a live card.

Membership “tiers” in Counter are loyalty benefits: a priority pickup buffer and campaigns scoped to a tier. They are a partner-network idea. The repo has no commercial price list and no SaaS packaging.

The business problem

A normal point of sale is built for a person at the counter. An agent that scrapes that screen guesses when the store is closed, when oat milk is 86’d, and when a cart is too large to pay on its own.

Traditional POS + AI

Screen or inbox → guess hours and caps
Failure → re-enter by hand → several ways to “paid”
  • Agent integration is screen scraping or a side inbox
  • Out of stock comes back as a dead end, then a person retypes the order
  • Hours, spend caps, and 86 status live on a screen the agent cannot read
  • More than one path can mark a ticket paid
  • State is polled or webhooked across systems that can drift

Counter

Shared checkout → quote with policy
Refusal code → retry, substitute, or wait → one path to paid
  • Staff and buyer agents use one engine, shaped for a machine
  • Out of stock, closed, and over cap come back as codes the agent can act on
  • Public policy is readable: issuer, scopes, hours, and the spend cap
  • Paid and kitchen-fired happen on one server path after payment succeeds
  • One store of record here. A sale pushed to another POS can still lag

Three product beats

The pitch is three moves. Each one is a business rule you can watch, and a product claim about what drops out when the rule holds: fewer guessed failures, one enforced path to paid, substitution the agent can finish, and one store the next quote already knows.

1. A buyer places the order

Same checkout a register would use, shaped for a machine. The agent discovers policy, asks for a quote, and pays on that ticket. It does not scrape a staff screen.

2. The store answers with recovery

Out of stock, closed, needs approval. The agent retries, substitutes, or stops, because the answer is a code it can read.

3. A person decides the money

Caps, comps, and refunds stay with a manager. Approval is a human control. The model and the browser never write paid.

What you actually use

Four surfaces. The landing page is the pitch and the try path. The register is the operator’s policy console. Text to order proves a person hits the same rules. Rewards shows when points are allowed to move.

Landing

Pitch, three beats, then a live strip: “Loading this location’s public policy…” Doors from there: Open the register, For POS, Text to order, For agents, Rewards. The first try is about three minutes: run the oat-milk recovery, then optionally the phone or the agent path.

Register

Operator console. Sign in, then a sandbox banner: FAKE PAY · SANDBOX. The trace stays empty until Run agent. Goals cover the oat-milk latte and the over-cap cart. Staff 86 items, Approve or Reject, and close the day. Help lists the halts: substitute when something is out of stock, stop when the store is closed. The demo agents are deterministic, so the run shows policy. A fuller staff ticket can ask for a comp or a refund in chat. That chat cannot approve one.

Text to order

Shelf tags and a handset. “Text a shelf code. PAY sends it. NO drops it.” Replies stay operational: “Paid. The register has it.” “… is 86’d. Not added.” “Store is closed. Not added.” “That total needs a manager. Not paid.”

Rewards

A merchant publishes a plan. A quote evaluates it and does not move points. Only the register commits them. The line on the guide: merchant files, and settlement stays on the register.

Three journeys

  1. Oat milk is 86’d. The buyer prefers a latte with oat. The store answers out of stock. The agent substitutes, the quote becomes ready, and fake pay completes. Staff do not have to retype the order. Un-86 and the original drink is quotable again.
  2. The cart is over the auto cap. Above the spend cap the buyer may pay alone, the ticket waits. Complete stays refused until a manager chooses Approve or Reject. The model cannot raise the cap.
  3. A shelf code is texted. PAY sends the same checkout. A closed store, an 86’d item, or an over-cap total comes back in the handset’s voice, and a paid ticket lands on the register.

Design decisions

One store
Staff and agents read the same counter. An 86 is on the next quote. A sale pushed downstream can still diverge. That limit is stated on the product page.
Approvals
Judgment stays in the operator UI. Comps, refunds, and over-cap carts are requested and then decided by a person. Chat can file. It cannot settle.
Flat menu
The buyer orders finished items. Modifiers are already in the catalog, so an agent cannot invent a drink the kitchen does not make.
Sandbox
Fake pay and a fixed demo clock make the trust boundary tryable. No card is charged. The clock stays put so hours and “ready by” do not drift under the visitor.
Downstream POS
Another register can receive the sale. It does not get to mark Counter’s ticket paid. Paid and kitchen-fired happen only after payment succeeds, on one server path.
This version
Scope is the counter and the sandbox. No certified ChatGPT or Google listing, no card-present hardware, no voice line, no offline mode. The written next step is one live merchant on Stripe.

What a later merchant adds

The sandbox is the product you can run now. The path already written for a live counter is a single merchant on Stripe, with the same rule: payment success is still the only way a ticket becomes paid.

PM takeaways

  • Agent commerce fails when the interface is a screen a model has to scrape
  • Trust is a surface you can see: the spend cap, the 86, and who is allowed to approve
  • Machines need a code they can act on. People need a halt they can decide
  • Settlement belongs on one path. Everything else can ask. One function writes paid
  • A sandbox with fake pay is how you show that boundary before a card is in the flow
